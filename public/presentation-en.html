Capability-matched work distribution

Work goes in a folder. The right name comes out.

A head of distribution drops a job into a folder and stops thinking about it. The system checks it against what every person on the team can actually do — recorded levels, certifications and their expiry dates, availability, workload — removes everyone who does not qualify, ranks the ones who do, and hands the work over with its reasoning attached.

Nobody unqualified is ever in the running Every decision explains itself Finished work stays attached to a person
What happens to one task

Four steps, in this order, every time

The order matters. Qualification is settled before ranking begins, so no score can ever talk the system into someone who should not be there.

STEP 1

The admin fills in the job

What the work is, how long it should take, when it is due, and which capabilities it needs. That is the last decision a person has to make.

STEP 2

Unqualified people are removed

Missing capability, level too low, expired certification, wrong language, on leave, no capacity left. Fail one and you are out — there is no score in this step.

STEP 3

The rest are ranked

Seven weighted factors: how far each person clears the bar, whether a lead verified it, experience, spare capacity, workload balance, the deadline, and role fit.

STEP 4

One person gets it — on the record

The assignment carries its own reasoning. Everyone who was passed over is listed with the exact requirement they missed.

The team

Five people, five different educations

This is what the system reads. Not a job title — a specification: what each person studied, what they are genuinely good at and to what level, which certificates they hold and when those run out, and every job they have finished.

A capability marked has been signed off by a lead. One that is only self-declared still counts, but it ranks below a verified one.

Why it matters

The point is not that a computer picks a name

The point is that an unqualified person can never receive a task, and that months later you can still show exactly why a particular job went to a particular person. Distribution stops being someone’s morning and becomes a record.

Same inputs, same answer

No randomness anywhere. Run this distribution a hundred times and it picks the same person a hundred times, in the same order.

Or it refuses to choose

When two people are genuinely inseparable, a strict folder sends the decision to a human rather than guessing. Refusing is a legitimate outcome.

Work spreads out

Workload balance is a ranking factor, so the strongest person does not quietly absorb every job in the queue.

Overrides are allowed — and logged

An admin can assign past a failed requirement, but only by writing down why. That reason is kept next to their name.

Every score, rejection reason and ranking on this page is real output from the matching engine, generated by running these five profiles and these two tasks through it — not written by hand.
